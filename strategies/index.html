Investment judgment across different kinds of real estate.

Land-Based Strategies is not organized around a single property type.

The focus is on situations where price, property characteristics, transaction complexity, information, structure, or execution creates a compelling investment thesis.

Land, distressed situations, assemblage, subdivision, commercial property, and selective development can all fit. Each opportunity must stand on its own.

What makes an opportunity worth investigating?

A price worth testing

An apparent discount is a starting point for research. The analysis needs to account for the property’s condition, constraints, costs, and realistic marketability.

A property characteristic that changes the thesis

Frontage, access, configuration, or the relationship to neighboring land can affect the usefulness of a site. Further research may reveal a different investment from the one initially advertised.

A transaction problem that can be understood

Ownership questions, judgments, delinquent taxes, or an approaching deadline may complicate a purchase. The work is to understand the situation and establish whether there is a practical path through it.

An information gap

A poorly presented opportunity may require assembling information from several sources. Research can clarify a thesis, change its economics, or provide a reason to stop.

Direct investment

Alexander’s direct investment experience includes sourcing opportunities, researching ownership and property conditions, negotiating acquisitions, coordinating with title professionals, arranging financing with investment partners, and carrying out physical improvements or land-configuration work.

Participation varies by transaction. Investments may be made independently or with partners.

Selective development and commercial investment

Development is considered where the site, demand, costs, approvals, capital requirements, and execution responsibilities support the thesis.

The same questions apply to commercial acquisitions and repositioning: what needs to change, who will do the work, what it will cost, and how the property will support a sale or continuing operation.

Ground-up development is a selective objective informed by experience, rather than a standing commitment to a particular product type or development volume.

Investing alongside other sponsors and operators

Investment activity can also include selective LP investments and capital placements in opportunities led by others.

In those situations, the property thesis is considered alongside the sponsor’s role, the proposed structure, capital requirements, and execution plan.

Time is part of the investment.

A purchase can appear attractive and still commit capital for longer than expected.

Pavonia was initially conceived as an approximately six-month investment. A dispute with the former owner extended the project to approximately two years before renovation and sale were complete.

That experience made duration and execution risk concrete. A business plan needs to account for the path through the investment, including what could delay it.

Texas is the principal geography.

Houston is the home base. The investment record includes work in Greater Houston, Waller County, the Hill Country, and Hunt County.

Other Texas opportunities are considered through local evidence. Willingness to evaluate a market does not replace knowledge of a particular property, its demand, and its constraints.

Bring the specifics.

For an initial conversation, useful information includes the property location, approximate size, current situation, proposed terms, and any deadline.

For an investment led by another sponsor, include the investment thesis, sponsor responsibilities, proposed structure, and timing.
